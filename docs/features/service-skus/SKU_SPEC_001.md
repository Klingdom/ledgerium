# Service SKU Specification 001 — Onboarding & Process Audit

**Date:** 2026-08-21
**Status:** DRAFT — pricing is a coordinator proposal, not a CEO decision
**Context:** `docs/meta/REVENUE_PLAN_20K_001.md` §6. CEO directed both SKUs 2026-08-21.
**Depends on:** the one-time payment (`mode: 'payment'`) capability being added to `api/billing/checkout`.

---

## Why these two, and not the obvious ones

The tempting "creative monetization" moves were assessed and rejected:

- **SOP template packs** — `docs/meta/REVENUE_PLAN_20K/pm_analysis.md` found these *"actively contradict the product's core not-inferred, not-generic differentiation."* Ledgerium's entire claim is that documentation comes from observed reality rather than a template someone wrote from memory. Selling templates would compete on the axis the product exists to escape, and would undercut the sentence that justifies $89 over $49.
- **Public API SKU** — `packages/api-client/` is empty. That is a quarter of engineering, not a SKU.

Both SKUs below **sell the moat rather than dilute it**: they are priced on deterministic analysis of the customer's own recorded evidence, which no competitor can produce.

---

## SKU 1 — Guided Onboarding

**Proposed price: $299 one-time.**

### The problem it solves
The install path is currently a Chrome Developer-mode sideload (the Web Store listing is prepared but not yet submitted). Even after that ships, the gap between "installed the extension" and "has recorded enough real work to get value" is where trials die. This SKU is paid activation.

### What the customer receives
1. A working install, verified live — not a link to instructions.
2. Two workflows recorded **with** them, on their real systems, not a demo.
3. Their first generated SOP and process map, reviewed together.
4. A short written recommendation of the next 3–5 workflows worth recording, chosen from what was observed.

### Explicitly out of scope
- Custom development, integrations, or API work.
- Documenting workflows on the customer's behalf beyond the two sessions.
- Ongoing support beyond the engagement (that is the subscription).

### Fulfilment
Two sessions, ≤60 minutes each, within 14 days of purchase. Delivered by a human. No automated component — the value is that someone competent watches them do it once.

### Pricing rationale
B2B SaaS onboarding is conventionally 1–3× monthly subscription value. At Solo ($89) this is ~3.4×; at Team ($249) ~1.2×. It is priced to be expensable without procurement — a corporate card decision, not a purchase order.

## SKU 2 — Process Audit

**Proposed price: $1,500 one-time.**

### The problem it solves
Ops leaders suspect a process is slow or inconsistent but have no evidence — only anecdote and a written SOP that may not match reality. This SKU answers that with measurement rather than opinion.

### What the customer receives
Every item below is computed by the existing intelligence engine from the customer's own recorded runs. **Nothing here is inferred, estimated by a human, or generated by an LLM.**

| Deliverable | Engine capability |
|---|---|
| Cycle-time distribution per step, with variance | `analyzeTimestudy`, `analyzeVariance`, `coefficientOfVariation` |
| Bottleneck identification, ranked | `detectBottlenecks` |
| Variant analysis — how many different ways the process is actually run | `buildVariantRecords`, `computeVariantDistance` |
| Recommended canonical path | `deriveRecommendedCanonicalPath` |
| Standardization score | `computeStandardizationScore` |
| Documentation drift — where the written SOP diverges from observed reality | `analyzeSopAlignment`, `computeDocumentationDriftScore` |
| Automation opportunity with ROI estimate | `computeAutomationROI`, `automationScorer` |
| Portfolio-level view across audited processes | `analyzePortfolio` |

Delivered as a written report plus a walkthrough session, with **every finding traceable to the source events that produced it**. That traceability is the differentiator and should be visible in the artifact itself.

### Scope boundary — the thing that keeps this productized
- **Up to 3 processes**, minimum 5 recorded runs each. Below 5 runs the variance and variant figures are not statistically meaningful, and the report must say so rather than present thin data as insight.
- One revision round.
- Fixed 10-business-day turnaround from the point recordings are complete.

### Explicitly out of scope
- Implementing the recommendations.
- Change management, training delivery, or stakeholder facilitation.
- Processes that cannot be browser-recorded (physical, phone-based, or offline work). This must be qualified **before** purchase, not discovered after.

### Pricing rationale
Process-consulting engagements run $2k–$20k+. At $1,500 this is deliberately below the threshold that triggers procurement in most mid-market orgs, while being high enough to fund real analyst time. It is a productized audit, not an open-ended engagement — the scope boundary above is what makes that true.

### Strategic value beyond revenue
`docs/meta/SEO_AEO_EFFECTIVENESS_REVIEW/competitive_analysis.md` found that **original, proprietary data is the strongest single driver of AI citation**, and that Ledgerium is currently absent from every third-party comparison surface. Audits generate exactly that: anonymized, aggregate findings about how real processes actually run. That is a genuine original-data asset — the kind of thing that earns citations and backlinks, which is the identified root cause of ranking at position 44.

**This SKU may be worth more as a content and authority engine than as direct revenue.**

## Honest risks

1. **Neither SKU scales.** Both consume human time. They are appropriate for the first 10–20 customers, where the constraint is proving value and learning what customers actually need — not for scaling to $20k MRR. They should be reassessed once recurring revenue exists.
2. **Audit quality depends on recording volume.** A customer who records 2 runs gets a report that must honestly say the data is insufficient. The 5-run minimum is a hard qualification gate, not a guideline — shipping thin analysis as insight would damage the trust the product is built on.
3. **Fulfilment capacity is one person.** At ~4 hours for onboarding and ~1–2 days for an audit, roughly 4 audits per month is the ceiling before it becomes the business rather than a supplement to it.
4. **Sequencing.** There are currently zero customers and Solo is not yet purchasable. These SKUs should not delay the shorter path: create the Stripe products, enable Portal plan switching, sell the subscription.

---

## Decisions required from the CEO

1. **Prices** — $299 and $1,500 are coordinator proposals based on the reasoning above, not findings. Both are one-line changes.
2. **Fulfilment owner** — both require a human. Who?
3. **Qualification gate for the audit** — who confirms the process is browser-recordable before payment is taken? Selling an audit for work that cannot be recorded is a refund and a bad review.
4. **Whether the audit's anonymized aggregate data may be used as published original research** (recommended, but it is a customer-data decision and belongs in the terms).
